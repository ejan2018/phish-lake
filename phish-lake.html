PHISH LAKE

Thirty big fish patrol the lake — and every single one is carrying mail from your inbox. Land them, read the messages inside — Discord, Google, Apple, Steam, your bank — and call each one: legitimate business, or bait on a hook?

01

Cast. Hold the mouse to charge, steer with the cursor, release to send the bobber across the golden water. Watch for the jumps — a leaping fish is a hungry fish.

02

Hook & reel. Strike the instant the float dips — even a tick can be hooked. Then obey the big letters: REEL when it tires, SLACK when it runs.

03

Classify. Inspect the recovered message — sender, links, attachments — then call it. Twelve catches per shift, badges for the sharp-eyed, and a printable certificate with your own photo at the end.

All messages are fictional training material. Any resemblance to real scams is, unfortunately, probable. Hover every sender, every link, every attachment — the clues are in the details.

Field Guide

Six habits of people who never take the bait.

Read the real addressDisplay names lie. The domain after the @ is the identity. Watch look-alikes: paypa1, micros0ft, lnstagram, d0cusign.
Hover every linkThe blue words and the true destination are different things. In this lake, hover to see where a link really goes.
Feel the pressure"Within 24 hours", "final warning", "account suspended". Urgency is a tool, not information.
Follow the secretsNo legitimate service asks for your password, PIN, CVV, gift-card codes or crypto recovery phrase over email.
Mind the attachmentsUnexpected .zip or executable files are malware's favorite outfit. A real invoice rarely travels zipped.
Cost your mistakesA missed phish steals data; a false report buries real work. Accuracy beats paranoia.
